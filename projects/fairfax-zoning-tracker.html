About the data

Source

Fairfax County Department of Planning and Development, through the county's public PLUS Zoning Applications dataset, which covers zoning applications submitted since 2000. District boundaries come from the county's Supervisor Districts dataset.

How it is pulled

Each time this page opens, your browser requests every case directly from the county's public ArcGIS REST service, in batches of up to 2,000 records. Nothing is copied or stored on this site, so the page shows the county's data as it stands when you load it.

Methods

To write: methods, in the Measures / Limitations pattern of your transit page The "About the data" box at the top already covers the source and how the data is pulled, so focus here on definitions and limitations. Points to cover:
  • Unit of analysis. What one record is (one application, including amendments filed as their own records).
  • Outcomes. How the county's 20-plus statuses are grouped into four outcomes (the table above shows the grouping), and how the approval rate is defined.
  • Time to decision. Days from submission to the status date of approved or denied cases. Impossible dates are excluded.
  • Districts. Assigned from each case's center point using current supervisor district boundaries.
  • Proposed use. Sorted from each case's written description and requested zoning district (the table above lists the rules). Say that it is approximate, and that plan amendments often do not state a use.
  • Limitations. The status date can reflect a later administrative update rather than the hearing date; the data has no unit counts; plan amendments (PCA, FDP, and similar) are tied to earlier rezonings; records begin in 2000. Also confirm whether cases in the towns of Herndon, Vienna, and Clifton appear, since the towns run their own zoning.

Tools and disclosure

JavaScript, Leaflet, and Fairfax County's public ArcGIS map services (PLUS zoning applications and supervisor districts). Basemap from OpenStreetMap and CARTO.
